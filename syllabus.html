International Business

Course Description

The course introduces students to key concepts of micro- and macroeconomics necessary for understanding the global business environment. It covers principles of supply and demand, market structures, macroeconomic indicators, economic policy, and international trade. Special attention is given to analyzing real-world economic cases in a business context.

Course Objectives

  • Understand Fundamental Economic Principles: Develop knowledge of key concepts in microeconomics (scarcity, supply and demand, consumer choice, production costs) and macroeconomics (GDP, inflation, unemployment, fiscal and monetary policy).
  • Analyze Individual and Aggregate Economic Behavior: Study how households and firms make decisions, and how these decisions interact to form markets on national and global levels.
  • Evaluate the Role of Government and Institutions: Understand how government policies (taxation, spending, regulation, monetary policy) influence efficiency, equity, and growth.
  • Apply Economic Models to Real-World Issues: Use theoretical models and empirical data to analyze real economic problems, such as inflation, recessions, international trade, and globalization.
  • Develop Critical Thinking and Policy Assessment Skills: Build the ability to critically assess economic policies, interpret indicators, and propose informed solutions for business decision-making.

Expected Learning Outcomes (LO)

LO 1

Macroeconomic Measurement

Understanding the methodology for measuring production activity of the national economy and interpreting economic performance ratings.

LO 2

Consumer & Firm Behavior

Explaining trends in consumer and firm behavior across various market structures, and analyzing factors affecting supply and demand.

LO 3

Global Market Assessment

Providing qualified economic assessment of current events in international and digital markets at a global level.

LO 4

Unemployment & Inflation Analysis

Understanding key causes of unemployment and inflation, and evaluating government policy responses influencing their dynamics.

LO 5

Market Failures & Government Role

Explaining market imperfections in specific scenarios and evaluating incentives for government policy intervention.

LO 6

Financial Markets & Interconnections

Deep understanding of how the macroeconomy and financial markets operate, tracing key causal linkages between them.

Course Politics & Conduct Rules

Class Ethics & Attendance

  • To complete the course, you must attend at least 70% of classroom sessions.
  • Punctuality is mandatory. Tardiness disrupts the class and late students will not be admitted.
  • Tasks missed due to valid reasons can be retaken during IWST by prior arrangement with the instructor.

Communication & Platonus

  • Students must check daily their Personal Page in Platonus AIS and corporate email (@almau.edu.kz).
  • All assignments must be submitted strictly on time via approved university platforms.
  • A respectful, collaborative, and friendly academic atmosphere is expected at all times.

Academic Withdrawal (AW)

  • Systematic violations of classroom conduct or academic honesty lead to an AW grade (Administrative Withdrawal), equivalent to F (GPA 0).
  • Cheating or unauthorized electronic devices during exams result in immediate work cancellation.

Inclusive Community & Support

  • AlmaU classrooms are safe spaces free from discrimination based on gender, race, or status. Bullying is zero-tolerated.
  • Student Support Center (SSC) and Advising Center provide guidance on educational plans and certificates.

Academic Integrity Standards (AlmaU)

Core Student Principles:

  1. Independent Work: All tasks, tests, and projects must be completed individually. Obvious signs of copying or shared solutions will result in "0" points for both students.
  2. Source Citation: Using external ideas, texts, or data requires explicit reference and citation acknowledging original contributors.
  3. Data Authenticity: Falsification or distortion of research indicators and empirical market data is strictly prohibited.

Disciplinary Sanctions for Violations:

1. Warning First minor offense (e.g., unintentional citation error).
2. Resubmission Correcting assignment considering academic standards.
3. Grade Reduction Cancellation or zeroing of points for current project/module.
4. Investigation Disciplinary committee, course suspension, or "F" grade.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy

Guidelines for using Large Language Models (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) in Principles of Economics.

PERMITTED AI Uses

  • Automating mathematical calculations or modeling economic data.
  • Assisting comprehension of complex microeconomic theories and graphs.
  • Source discovery, research assistance, and grammar proofreading.
  • Practicing sample test questions with interactive AI feedback.

PROHIBITED AI Uses

  • Replacing personal critical thinking, analysis, and economic reasoning with AI text.
  • Fully generating essay submissions, case analyses, or report texts without student input.
  • Automated submission of AI-generated content, which is classified as **academic misconduct**.
